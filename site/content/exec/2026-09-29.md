## The Week at a Glance

- 🔴 **EvilTokens phishing platform is stealing employee credentials at scale** - a disrupted but still-active Phishing-as-a-Service operation uses AI-generated lures to steal authentication tokens, bypassing passwords and multi-factor authentication entirely; organizations without phishing-resistant MFA and Conditional Access policies face immediate account-takeover risk.
- 🔴 **Storm-3168 is actively attacking Azure cloud accounts via compromised app identities** - attackers linked to a known threat group are using stolen service principal credentials to probe, delete resources, and harvest credentials inside Azure environments, a direct threat to cloud infrastructure integrity and business continuity.
- 🟡 **AI agents are proliferating faster than governance frameworks** - employees can now build and share Copilot agents with one prompt, and without agent inventory, access controls, and tool-blocking policies in place, organizations face growing data-exposure and compliance risk as the agent estate expands.
- 🟢 **Microsoft is broadly upgrading its Copilot and device management platforms** - new capabilities across Intune, SharePoint, and the Copilot app are rolling out this month and next, bringing productivity improvements that require light IT readiness but no urgent decisions.

## Why This Week Matters

Two confirmed, active threat campaigns this week target the specific trust assumptions most organizations have not yet closed: device code authentication flows that bypass MFA, and cloud application identities that carry elevated permissions with little ongoing scrutiny. At the same time, the rapid expansion of employee-built AI agents is creating a shadow estate of automated systems that can access email, calendars, SharePoint, and third-party services, often without IT visibility. Leadership needs to understand that the identity perimeter and the AI governance perimeter are now the same conversation.

## Risk & Compliance

- 🔴 **[EvilTokens device code phishing campaign](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/22/unmasking-eviltokens-getting-to-the-root-of-device-code-phishing/):** {{< emphasis "Identity" >}} This Phishing-as-a-Service platform uses AI-generated lures to steal authentication tokens and bypass MFA, giving attackers persistent access to Microsoft 365 accounts without needing a password; this directly threatens HIPAA, SOC 2, and CMMC access-control requirements, and cyber insurance policies that mandate phishing-resistant authentication - act immediately to restrict device code flow and enforce Conditional Access.

- 🔴 **[Storm-3168 cloud identity attacks on Azure](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/25/storm-3168-agentic-driven-cloud-attacks-using-compromised-service-principals/):** {{< emphasis "Identity" >}} Attackers are actively exploiting compromised service principals (the machine identities that connect apps and automation to Azure) to delete resources and steal credentials, exploiting an implicit-trust gap that most organizations leave unmonitored; this is a NIST CSF Protect and Detect control failure, and affected organizations may face cloud outage, data loss, and breach notification obligations.

- 🔴 **[Storm-2570 ransomware affiliate using consistent cross-platform tradecraft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/24/beyond-ransomware-tracking-storm-2570-consistent-tradecraft-across-deployments/):** This group deploys multiple ransomware families (Qilin, DragonForce, Anubis, BERT) using the same post-compromise playbook across victims, meaning defenders who know the pattern can disrupt attacks before encryption begins; organizations without endpoint detection and response coverage should treat this as an urgent gap given active deployments.

- 🔴 **[NeedyMantis post-compromise malware framework](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/28/needymantis-unpacking-a-post-compromise-malware-family-used-in-targeted-operations/):** {{< emphasis "SecOps" >}} This modular malware is designed to survive inside a network long after initial compromise, using encrypted components and extensible modules to evade detection and maintain access for follow-on operations; organizations without behavioral endpoint detection or network segmentation face prolonged undetected intrusions, a direct trigger for breach disclosure under HIPAA, GDPR, and state privacy laws.

- 🟡 **[AI agent governance gap as employee-built agents proliferate](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/copilot-studio-blog/governance-is-becoming-agentic-too-how-enterprises-can-operate/ba-p/4556820/):** {{< emphasis "AI" >}} Employees can now create Copilot agents in a single prompt and share them across teams, granting those agents access to email, calendars, Teams conversations, and SharePoint content - without IT visibility unless agent inventory and access controls are configured; this creates a data-exposure risk relevant to HIPAA minimum-necessary rules, GDPR data minimization, and SOC 2 access management controls.

- 🟡 **[MCP server tool-blocking controls now available in Agent 365](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/PuGUfwpaHFU):** {{< emphasis "AI" >}} Organizations can now centrally block agents from connecting to risky or unauthorized MCP servers (the external connectors that let agents take actions in third-party apps), but this control only protects you if administrators actively review and govern the tool catalog; plan a policy review within 30 days as agent adoption grows.

- 🟡 **[Purview auto-labeling capacity increase to 500,000 files per day](https://www.microsoft.com/microsoft-365/roadmap?id=571309):** {{< emphasis "Data" >}} Microsoft is raising the ceiling on automatic sensitivity labeling in SharePoint and OneDrive, arriving November 2026, which gives organizations a material opportunity to close data-classification gaps before Copilot accesses that content; organizations with HIPAA, GDPR, or CMMC obligations should plan to expand labeling policies to cover previously untagged files.

- 🟢 **[Microsoft Cloud PKI now available for GCC High environments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/intune/cloud-pki/):** Government contractors and regulated agencies in GCC High can now manage device certificate infrastructure entirely in the cloud, removing a common on-premises dependency; this strengthens device-trust verification and supports CMMC and FedRAMP certificate-based authentication requirements with no new hardware.

- 🟢 **[Chromium browser vulnerabilities patched in Microsoft Edge](https://msrc.microsoft.com/update-guide/vulnerability/CVE-2026-87536):** Multiple high-severity flaws in the browser engine underlying Edge were disclosed and patched this week, including memory corruption and race condition bugs; organizations managing Edge through Intune should confirm automatic updates are enabled, as unpatched browsers remain a common initial access vector.

- **Planner looks different.** Microsoft Planner has a cleaner, modernized interface rolling out now, with improved navigation and a new Goals feature in basic plans. Users working with scanned contracts or image-heavy presentations will notice meaningfully better search results starting in October.

- **Teams call history is reorganizing for multi-line users.** Employees who manage multiple phone lines in Teams will see a redesigned call history with separate tabs per line, arriving in November. This is a positive change for those users, but brief training notes for your telephony-heavy teams will help.
- **Copilot agents are appearing in shared workspaces.** As colleagues build and share Copilot agents, employees will see them in Copilot Chat, Teams, and the Agent Store. Help desk and communications teams should be prepared for questions about what these agents can access and who authorized them.

## If You Take No Action

**EvilTokens token-theft campaign:** Without restricting device code authentication flows and enforcing phishing-resistant MFA, your organization remains fully exposed to a live, scaled attack that bypasses your current MFA investment entirely. A compromised account does not require a password - attackers hold a valid session token and can access email, files, and connected systems until that token expires or is revoked. The downstream consequences include data exfiltration, regulatory breach notification obligations, and the reputational and financial cost of an incident that was preventable with controls already available in your existing Microsoft licensing.

**Storm-3168 service principal compromise:** Service principals are the automated identities behind your cloud integrations, workflows, and deployed applications. Without auditing their permissions and monitoring for anomalous behavior, an attacker who obtains a single compromised credential can traverse your Azure environment, delete resources, and pivot to additional accounts - potentially causing sustained cloud outages or data destruction. Organizations in regulated industries face additional exposure: a cloud resource deletion event may constitute a breach or business continuity failure triggering contractual, regulatory, or cyber-insurance notification requirements.

**AI agent governance gap:** Every Copilot agent a colleague builds and shares can be granted access to organizational email, Teams conversations, SharePoint files, and third-party systems. Without a governance policy, your organization has no reliable way to know what agents exist, what data they can reach, or whether they have been shared with unauthorized users. As the agent estate grows, the window for establishing controls narrows - retrofitting governance onto a large, undocumented agent population is significantly harder and more disruptive than defining the rules before widespread adoption takes hold.
